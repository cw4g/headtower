# Settings

Settings holds the things you touch rarely but need to trust completely - the
keys that let devices and tooling onto the network, and the diagnostics that tell
you Headtower and Headscale agree.

## Pre-auth keys

**Pre-auth keys** let a device join the tailnet without an interactive login -
the basis for automated and headless enrollment. Headtower lets you:

- **create** keys, choosing whether they are reusable or single-use, ephemeral or
  persistent, and pre-tagged,
- see each key's **state and expiry** at a glance,
- **revoke** a key the moment it should no longer enroll anything.

<Callout type="warning">
  A pre-auth key is a credential to join your network. Prefer short-lived,
  single-use, tagged keys, and revoke anything you are unsure about.
</Callout>

## API keys

**API keys** are how tooling - including Headtower itself - authenticates to
Headscale's API. The Settings view lists keys with their expiry so nothing
outlives its usefulness unnoticed, and supports rotating them on a schedule you
control. Secrets are shown once, at creation, and never again.

## Diagnostics

The diagnostics panel confirms Headtower can reach Headscale and reports what it
found - the control plane it is pointed at, the version it is talking to, and any
mismatch worth knowing about. When something is off, this is the first place to
look: it states the problem plainly rather than failing in the background.

## Trust, not chrome

Settings is intentionally quiet. There are no toggles for their own sake - every
control here changes who or what can reach the network, or tells you whether the
console is seeing the truth. That is exactly the kind of surface that should be
calm and unambiguous.
